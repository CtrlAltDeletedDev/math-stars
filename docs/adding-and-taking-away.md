# Adding and taking away: how they climb

This is for a parent. It describes how the app moves a child from "+1" to fluent facts
within 20, and what changed in the v7 redesign.

## The steps

Each step is one rung of the Adding or Taking Away ladder. They move up a rung after
6 of their last 8 answers at it are right (or after their first 4 at a rung are all right,
while the app is still finding their level), and moves down after 3 or fewer of 8.

| # | Adding | Taking away | What they are learning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | +1 | −1 | One more and one less: the next number, the number before |
| 2 | +2 | −2 | Counting on and back two |
| 3 | within 5 | within 5 | Small facts, including +0 |
| 4 | **count on 1, 2, 3** | **count back 1, 2, 3** | Mixed, and turned around: 2 + 9 is 9 + 2 |
| 5 | within 10 | within 10 | All the facts to 10 |
| 6 | **ten and some more** | **take away from teens** | 10 + 4 is a ten and 4 ones; 14 − 4 and 14 − 10 |
| 7 | **doubles, near doubles** | **take away with doubles** | 6 + 6, then 6 + 7 is "a double and one more"; 12 − 6 |
| 8 | **adding 9** | **taking away 9 and 8** | 9 is one away from 10: 9 + 6 is 10 + 5 |
| 9 | **make a ten** | **back through ten** | 8 + 5 → 8 + 2 + 3; 13 − 5 → 13 − 3 − 2 |
| 10 | within 20 | within 20 | Everything, mixed |

The bold rows are new. Before them, the ladder jumped from "within 10" straight to
"within 20", and most of within 20 is facts that cross ten. Those need a strategy, and
counting can't stand in for one. A child who was fine at "within 10" would start getting
most of them wrong and drop back down, again and again.

First grade tops out at step 10. Kindergarten tops out at step 5. Second grade keeps going
to within 100 and 200.

## What they see

- **Ten frames, not loose dots.** 7 is "5 and 2 more" at a glance. 8 + 5 fills the first
  frame and spills 3 into the second, which is the make-a-ten strategy drawn out.
- **The picture fades.** On a step they're new to, the picture is always there. Once they're
  getting it right, it waits behind a **Show me** button. If they tap a wrong answer it
  comes straight back. Tapping it is never held against them.
- **A hint that names the strategy.** After a wrong first tap they get one more try and a
  hint like "Turn it around! Start on the bigger number" or "Make a ten! 8 needs 2 more".
  The hint never gives the answer away.

## Practising while you're busy

- **Plus & Minus**, the big button on the home screen, is just sums: adding and taking away
  mixed together, two of each in every four questions, in rounds of 20. It moves them up and
  down by itself. Every fourth question is a warm-up from a step they have already passed, so
  older facts stay fresh. About three rounds is 10–15 minutes.
- **Mix**, the smaller button under it, is the old Play: two topics they can climb plus one for
  variety each round.
- The Parent screen (triple-tap the "Math Stars!" title) opens with a **Plus & Minus** card: the
  step they're on in each, what comes next, how accurate and how quick they are there, and how many
  questions they did today.
- To drill one thing ("they're on +9 this week"), open **Adding**, then play the matching
  lesson from the strip (for example **Adding 9**).
- The Parent screen shows which step they're on in each topic, how accurate they are there, and
  which mistakes they keep making.
